# Estimated crow walking: initial instrument and visual trial

Question: can the existing folded-wing crow show alternating, grounded steps
and coordinated whole-body motion without changing accepted simulator states?
The current standing rig plants both feet. This is a visualization trial, not a
walking controller, physical contact experiment, or crow-specific calibration.

The qualitative gait starts with alternating left/right phases, a 62% stance
fraction, a 5 cm foot excursion and a 2 cm swing clearance. These are design
estimates, not measured American crow values. Corvid research reports low-speed
walking and faster hopping: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7998.1983.tb05784.x.
Avian gait research describes stationary feet during stance and moving feet
during swing: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.zool.2011.07.002. Hold the simulator
state and other presentation modes fixed. A follow-camera walking-in-place
capture represents relative leg movement with an implied constant forward
speed; it is not a simulated path or a validated animal gait.

Before visual acceptance, test step alternation, planted-toe no-slip relative
to the implied constant-speed body translation, toe clearance, stable limb
lengths, continuity across phase boundaries and a closed loop. Check side,
front and rear captures for penetration, limb disconnection and deformation of
body/feathers. Keep the gait opt-in and record failed trials. Separately examine
how accepted flight and contact signals can support takeoff and landing; never
substitute this estimated walk for a physical controller.
