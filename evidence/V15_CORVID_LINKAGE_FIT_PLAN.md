# Crow wing linkage reference: data fit and holdout, 2026-11-15

The current v15 body has one rigid mass per wing, with shoulder sweep, flap and pronation, and cannot physically fold at an elbow or wrist. The archived Baliga et al. *Corvus caurinus*-group cadaver data provide paired three-dimensional landmark-derived elbow and manus angles during a hand-manipulated linkage trial; original specimen filename `Cor_bra` differs from the author's taxon label, so identity must remain qualified. These data describe manual range of motion, not active flight, balancing, muscle forces or a living wing's natural frequency. Plane departures show that a single planar chain cannot exactly reconstruct the observed three-dimensional landmark poses.

As an exploratory, reversible intermediate toward an articulated physical model, fit the measured manus angle as a quadratic in measured elbow angle using only valid source frames 1–350 of the 17-1L linkage recording. Hold out all valid frames 351–502 from that same recording, and evaluate held-out absolute error, RMS error and sampled angle coverage; compare against the training-mean constant baseline. Pre-register an exploratory criterion of held-out RMS <15 degrees and improvement of at least 25% over the constant baseline, without refitting on held-out frames. Report out-of-training-range samples and all failures. Record polynomial coefficients in the paper's measured-angle convention only; do not infer 3D joint axes, hard limits, spring forces, continuous flight behavior or American-crow-specific calibration from this two-angle regression. A fit passing this screen is at most a soft prior for a later physically articulated model and needs separate specimen/layout validation and full flight requalification.
