# Earlier, coordinated leg feedback screen

The eight matched isolated hip, wing, ankle and knee feedback arms applied bounded corrections after +0.20 rad pitch. None produced a recovery; both hip signs and most leg signs advanced the loss of balance, while wing changes were negligible. Across the first 30 intervals after activation, each tested perturbation increased pitch versus the fixed-posture control. The retained trajectory shows the body reference point moving forward relative to the perch well before the feet lose upward impulse. The v15 sagittal hip, knee and ankle joints use parallel local pitch axes, motivating a distinct test that moves adjacent joints oppositely to limit their combined orientation change. Kinematic axis alignment alone does not establish a stabilizing response.

If tilt reaches 0.24 rad, instead require no termination or tilt >=0.35, the same continuous contacts, and an actual return to <0.20 rad for 50 consecutive intervals. A stabilization-only pass is not recovery: test explicit opposite-signed disturbances separately. Any passing arm also requires the separate easy-start comparison and then learning and teacher-off deployment before a neural claim.
