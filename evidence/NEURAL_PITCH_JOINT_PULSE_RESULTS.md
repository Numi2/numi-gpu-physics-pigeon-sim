# Matched hip and ankle pulse response

On consumed seed 20310129, the four-update v5-slow controller pitches forward and later falls. Four matched trials applied a five-interval +/-0.004 rad position-target pulse through both hips or both ankles; all other parameters were held fixed. Every full replay frame before interval 200 matched the retained baseline exactly, and the first changed q/v/action appeared at interval 201. This confirms matching prefixes and local physical response, not a stable controller.

At interval 201, the +hip and -hip pulses changed pitch by -0.00142 and +0.00156 rad versus baseline; +ankle and -ankle changed it by -0.00038 and +0.00062 rad. At interval 300, all four were instead more forward-pitched than baseline by 0.0058-0.0117 rad, after the perturbed state and neural observations had diverged. Thus the immediate response has a direction, but these brief pulses do not demonstrate a sustained corrective law. Each variant's longest strict qualifying streak was 202 intervals (42-243); all four were still running at the 300-step cutoff, before baseline termination at 322. By interval 299, both rear toe normal impulses were positive rather than opposed to the front contacts in all variants. Support impulses, normal impulses and six mean joint efforts at sampled intervals are retained in the JSON next to exact replay hashes; all 16 substeps are present in the archived traces.

The experiment uses a partially reconstructed branch fixture, scripted terminal carrier and learned residual; it is not evidence of complete autonomous landing or biological qualification. Raw observations, including the eventual baseline failure, are preserved in Git; no post-reset data is used for qualification.
