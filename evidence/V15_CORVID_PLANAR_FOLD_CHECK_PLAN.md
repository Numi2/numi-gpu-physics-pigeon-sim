# Corvid measured-wing planar approximation check

The v15 wing contains no independently modeled elbow or wrist, and its single body also carries the entire aerodynamic wing role. Measured four-point corvid cadaver landmarks indicate non-coplanarity. Before choosing a joint topology, quantify what a best-case planar three-segment skeleton would miss on the same linkage trial's reserved frames 351–502. Use each frame's measured segment lengths and the authors' measured 3D elbow and manus angles, allowing either possible planar fold branch and scoring the smaller absolute error in shoulder-to-distal-manus distance. This is an optimistic per-frame geometric lower bound for a planar model, not a dynamically realizable fitted controller. Do not select a branch from future motion or claim frequency, dynamics, aerodynamic accuracy, or native physics validity.

Report the error distribution in the source coordinate units, as a fraction of total within-frame segment length, and preserve invalid/degenerate frames. Compare training frames 1–350 only as context; do not fit parameters on the reserved portion. The result will indicate whether a planar approximation is adequate at a prospective 2% median normalized reach error; it will not validate any specific multiaxis design.
