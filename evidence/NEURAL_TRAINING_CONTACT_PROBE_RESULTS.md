# Traced training contact failure

On the first seven four-lane v4 training rollouts, all 92 complete episodes ended with contact reason 3; their median length was 138 steps, maximum 162. The eighth added 12 contact terminations. No completed episode in the first seven reached 300 steps. A separate one-lane 256-step probe used the first v4 training configuration and its original v3 starting policy, with the instrumented contact observer enabled. Its first transition termination was contact reason 3 at interval 148; the observer's corresponding host step 149 recorded a nonzero lower-beak (body 18) versus ground (body 35) contact starting at substep 6. The last positive-impulse perch contacts in the preceding descent were rear-toe distal bodies 26 and 34 versus perch body 40 at host step 144. The branch contacts then ceased. Recorded phase samples and all 16 substep contact, toe velocity, body-joint velocity and actuation traces are in the archive.

This identifies a real fall in this one instrumented probe. It does not prove every v4 contact termination involved the same collider pair, or that the injected noise caused the fall. Training sampled action slots 15–17 even though they were not trained, alongside the first four learned residuals. The separately registered matched exploration experiment checks that hypothesis. The observer q/v fields were empty during training; phase and joint diagnostic samples must not be mislabeled as a complete state trace. The existing video and deterministic evaluation establish different development fixtures and do not prove this stochastic training is stable.
