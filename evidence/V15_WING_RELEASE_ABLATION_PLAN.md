# Test whether tilt-triggered wing release amplifies the failure

Both signs of the same brief bilateral hip-target pulse caused forward pitch and eventual termination on the fixed -0.90-hip, lowered-wing hard-start fixture. Pitch began drifting before 0.22 radians and accelerated after the existing wing target started fading back toward deployed. That timing motivates, but does not establish, a causal role for the wing release.

Repeat the already-recorded positive and negative pulse trials, holding the same -0.4-radian physical flap offset whenever both feet remain loaded instead of fading it as pitch increases, beginning at episode step 3000. Maintain all other drives, limits, checkpoint, target pulse, support gating and 16-substep contact diagnostics. Verify separately shader-bound checkpoint parity and exact q/v/target agreement with each matching pulse control until the first flap target difference. No root force or contact modification is allowed; these are diagnostic mechanical interventions on a consumed reconstructed perch.

Report the first flap target change and matching preceding state, pitch divergence, first termination, bilateral upward branch impulse and toe-opposition proxy for each first episode. A plausible stabilizing result requires a >=0.02-radian induced pitch deviation followed by a return within 0.01 radians of the unperturbed reference for 50 continuous intervals, no termination for 1000 intervals, and continuous bilateral upward impulse and opposed-toe proxy. An earlier or later failure helps isolate causality but is not a pass. The test neither demonstrates that feathers physically fold nor qualifies autonomous landing or neural recovery.
