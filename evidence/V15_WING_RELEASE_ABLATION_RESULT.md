# Wing release ablation: no recovery

Holding the physical wing-lowering target through the induced tilt did not restore balance. The positive and negative pulse arms first terminated at intervals 696 and 735, versus 693 and 727 with the tilt-triggered wing release. The first altered flap targets appeared at intervals 562 and 555, respectively, and q/v matched the corresponding pulse controls exactly before those changes. Neither arm returned within 0.01 radians of the unperturbed pitch for 50 intervals after its excursion. Each reached more than one radian of pitch difference before first termination.

Both feet had net upward impulse throughout each first episode, while the opposed-toe-normal proxy persisted for 648/696 and 686/735 intervals. This is a contact-geometry proxy, not proof of grasp. For these two internal disturbances, suppressing wing release delayed termination only 3 and 8 intervals; it did not produce recovery. This diagnostic result is not a neural policy, external-push qualification, physical feather folding, or autonomous landing.
