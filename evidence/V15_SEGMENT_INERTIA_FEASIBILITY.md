# Wing-segmentation inertia feasibility

An inspector compiled against the v15 model reports each current distal wing body as 0.03574169 kg, with local center of mass (0, 0, 0) and Ixx = 0.0000368627 kg m². Its single collision shape and single aerodynamic role represent the whole wing. This is an estimated research model, not a direct measurement of the crow.

As one deliberately simplified feasibility calculation, take the measured humerus, ulna and carpometacarpus lengths of the single published common raven specimen, scale them by the cube root of the ratio between v15 body mass and that specimen's mass, place three equal point masses at those segment midpoints on a straight line, and distribute the current v15 wing mass equally. The parallel-axis contribution alone is 0.0001984965 kg m², 5.38 times the current v15 body Ixx; a pairwise identity independently gives the same result. Actual segments also have intrinsic moments. This is **not** a crow measurement or proof that every valid mass distribution violates the old inertia: segment masses and centroids were assumed and a single scaled raven is not a calibrated American crow.

The calculation exposes why preserving the old rigid body's inertia blindly while adding anatomical spans and mass may be physically inconsistent. An articulated replacement needs an explicit, positive mass and inertia model, segment collision and aerodynamic representations, and new flight and balance qualification. It must not silently reuse the old checkpoint or be represented as a cosmetic fold. Inspector source, build command, raw output, data and reproducible calculation are retained in `corvid-wing-morphology-data-20261115`; source attribution and uncertainties are in its README.
