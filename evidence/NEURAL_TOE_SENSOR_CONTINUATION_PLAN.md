# Toe-sensor continuation experiment

The matched one-update comparison established that the combined critic/actor clipping scales the actor gradient, but in this Adam update it did not materially increase the physical action change. On one development arrival, both the inherited and sensor-trained policies still fall. A single low-rate update is not a meaningful test of whether these new physical inputs can support learning.

Continue the separately clipped sensor-only candidate for four fixed-rate PPO updates, using four environments and 512 intervals per update, gamma .999, lambda .995 and two epochs. Use new training seeds 20271201 through 20271204 and retain every rollout, checkpoint, optimizer state, log, source, hash and failure. Resume the candidate's matching optimizer rather than silently starting Adam again. An isolated worker permits this one resume case; verify on every update that every inherited actor weight, bias, normalization and exploration value is unchanged and stop on drift. The twelve sensor input columns alone may change in the actor; the critic may learn.

Compare first-episode branch support and falls on the previously consumed development starts against the starting candidate. Retain earlier checkpoints if continuation regresses. No adaptive learning rate, test-seed tuning, claim of branch-only sensing or claim of learned toe grip is made. Contacts in the observations are external contact forces on toe body groups; the separate strict evaluator checks actual opposing branch contact. The carrier, reconstructed arrival and forced terminal mode remain scripted fixture limitations.
