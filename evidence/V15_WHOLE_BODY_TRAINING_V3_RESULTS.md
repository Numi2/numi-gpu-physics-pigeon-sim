# Whole-body v3 instrument and four-update training

The v3 null-control prefix and 50 resumed frames exactly matched the original recorded q, v, observations, actions, body states, rewards, outcomes and terminations. Its known physical pulse changed all 13 intended joint targets by the expected amounts. Training-shader prefixes exactly reproduced both original physically reached starts (150 hard frames, 50 easy). With a native stochastic fork on the same hard-start observation, all 13 bounded target deltas matched the sampled actions within 1.3e-8 radians; a separate positive hip-roll action changed left/right hip-roll targets by +0.08/-0.08 radians and left the beak target unchanged. Changes in actual joint velocity were recorded. These are instrument and actuator tests, not proof of useful balance or biological calibration.

Four PPO updates trained only actor output rows 8–17 through the existing 42-unit body-motion/contact feature path. The earlier leg/toe outputs and all other actor parameters passed exact protection guards after every update; critic updates were separate. Native/full-18-channel behavior log-probability reconstruction errors were at most 1.72e-5. The first-episode sample counts were 77, 104, 80 and 112; the complete 256-sample rollouts and all post-done samples remain in the archive. On update-four retained observations the protected output difference was exactly zero and new output RMS shifts ranged from 0.00159 to 0.00655 normalized units. Per-channel post-update KL and actor/critic clipped gradient norms are in stdout. These measurements establish that the new actor outputs changed, not that the crow can balance.

The physical evaluation is recorded separately. Both training starts are consumed development states with a reconstructed, relocated branch and scripted carrier; coupled bilateral joints and grouped toes do not provide independent control of every joint. Full shader and learner source, native probes, per-substep traces, checkpoints, all policy/optimizer/rollout files, guard checks, hashes, and commands are retained in the companion archive.
