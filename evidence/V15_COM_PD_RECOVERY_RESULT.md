# COM position and time-differenced rate feedback: no recovery

Both signed trials resumed shader-specific checkpoints with 49-frame q/v, actor-observation, action, body-state, reward and outcome parity. Through the first altered targets, they matched the pulse-only control in q/v, actor observations, accepted actions and body states. At step 563 the independent replay/contact calculation predicted hip changes of -0.0000037 and +0.0000037 rad; actual changes were +0.000318 and -0.000318 rad. The absolute errors satisfy the preregistered 0.004-rad sensor tolerance, but the signals are near zero and the signs differ, so these tiny initial values alone do not establish accurate feedback direction. The live COM position and finite-difference velocity had passed their separate diagnostic bounds.

The positive sign first terminated at control step 654, versus step 693 for the uncorrected pulse control; it had bilateral net upward impulse in only 618/654 intervals and opposed-toe-normal proxy in 602/654. The negative sign terminated at 693, with bilateral upward impulse in 693/693 and toe proxy in 650/693. Neither returned from the induced pitch excursion to within 0.01 rad of the unperturbed trajectory for 50 consecutive intervals. These fixed, bounded hip controls did not establish recovery; unchanged termination for one sign is not evidence of equal trajectories or effective balance.

The 20 ms finite-difference estimate uses source-identified v15 masses and idealized branch contact information. This research shader repurposes the episode tracking accumulator and invalidates that reported evidence. The test is an internally commanded hip perturbation on a consumed reconstructed branch, not external-push recovery, learned whole-body balance, continuous landing or performance on unused layouts.
