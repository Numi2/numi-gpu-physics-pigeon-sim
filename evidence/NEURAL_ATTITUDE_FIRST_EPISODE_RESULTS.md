# First-episode-only body-motion training comparison

An isolated copy of the MLX learner retained only samples through and including the first done of a single-lane, feedforward rollout. Advantages were calculated before slicing and stop at done. The complete native rollout remains retained. Four 256-step collections and PPO updates alternated the same reconstructed hard and easy physical starts, continuing the same saved four-update actor and optimizer as the extended-training experiment; learning rate was 0.0003, gamma 0.999 and lambda 0.995. Guard checks confirm protected actor parameters and optimizer moments were unchanged.

The initial full rollout matched the corresponding unfiltered-training rollout byte-for-byte, SHA-256 d2a365ee0fa45a922ed70cfa96f6d4720bdd5b49e77d34f52e006ced56a720c8. The updates used respectively 98, 154, 98 and 151 first-episode samples and excluded 158, 102, 158 and 105 post-done samples. Subsequent rollouts may differ after the first policy update, so this is a matched initial comparison rather than a fixed-data four-update study.

On the difficult start, the candidate fell at absolute step 256 (5.12 seconds), versus step 257 (5.14 seconds) for the original actor. On the easier start neither fell in 250 resumed steps; maximum tilt was 0.13094 versus 0.12947 radians for the original. These tests do not establish long-duration balance or independent learned toe grip. All starts were consumed in development. The branch is relocated and the phase and carrier are scripted; no autonomous landing is established. Full rollouts, actor packs, optimizer states, protected-weight guards, trial traces, scripts and the isolated learner code are retained alongside this report.
