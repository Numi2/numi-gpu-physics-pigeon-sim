# Repair candidate for wing mass and collider frames

Independent FP64 kinematics showed the v15 pronation child anchor is at the wing body's old COM origin; the previous candidate mistakenly used the flap proxy's offset as if it were the wing's shoulder coordinate. Consequently its upper collider extended inboard through the torso. The initial-contact diagnostic found no manifolds in unchanged v15 but four in the candidate: body/left upper, body/right upper (up to 7.91 mm penetration), and the two forearm/hand contacts at zero separation.

Construct a new versioned candidate without collision exclusions or suppressed contacts. Place each upper wing COM half a proposed upper-segment length outboard of the existing pronation joint by updating its child anchor; use a COM-centred upper collider and elbow anchor at the outboard half length. Keep the proposed segment masses, lengths and shape cross sections. Inset the forearm distal collision endpoint and hand proximal collision endpoint by 1 mm each, leaving the actual joint anchors and inertias intact; this is an explicitly approximate joint-clearance collider, not measured crow geometry. Preserve all pre-repair source and results.

Prediction for the same zero-gravity, zero-effort default pose: one 0.125 ms temporal-cone step has no candidate manifolds, no failed steps and maximum absolute generalized velocity below 0.0001; ten steps at 1 ms with eight substeps have no failed steps and maximum absolute velocity below 0.0001. Both wing chains must remain connected, body mass unchanged, and a separate zero-vs-elbow-effort free-motion test must still show a finite directed response and reaction. Compare exactly to the saved pre-repair run and archive results even if false. Passing does not qualify folding range, realistic mass distribution, self-clearance over the full range, flight, or biological accuracy.
