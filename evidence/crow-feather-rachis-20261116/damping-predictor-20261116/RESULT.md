# Damping predictor diagnostic

The matched native free step supports the split-integrator hypothesis in these controls. Across the three fixed nodes and three axes, the free predictor's published velocity agreed with `exp(-c*h)*(x_free-x_start)/h` within 1.30e-9 m/s at c=0 and 1.08e-9 m/s at c=100. The fixed-support velocity predicted from that relationship and exact return to the starting position agreed with the retained constrained output within 2.35e-6 m/s and 2.85e-6 m/s respectively, meeting the 1e-5 m/s diagnostic bound. Including the measured position error, the correction identity matches within 4.53e-9 and 1.89e-9 m/s.

Thus the damped candidate's position correction can leave a nonzero support velocity because its free predictor damps the reported velocity after updating position, while the world correction applies an undamped velocity change to position. This identifies a mechanism; it does not validate a fix, the finite-difference force residual, other attachments, CCD, contact, or physical feathers. Source and native binary/library/shader hashes are recorded. The free test was run on the current research source revision `1326ac525`; the attached retained trajectory came from base `512fbcde6719ef08a4317d204fe5fb94da0b3203` plus the archived experimental exact-support patch. The rod predictor and world correction used for this comparison were unchanged between them.
