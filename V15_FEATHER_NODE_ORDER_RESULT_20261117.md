# Fixed-support node-order control

The preregistered node-order prediction passed as a diagnostic, not as a solver qualification. Reversing the node order of the same straight, isotropic rod while holding its physical geometry and support targets fixed changed the physically mapped final displacement by up to 0.3187 micrometres. The forward case's node-1 minus node-7 displacement was +0.3744 micrometres; after mapping the reversed case back into physical order it was -0.00724 micrometres. The forward endpoint elastic-force difference was 0.05904 N; the reversed case's was about 0.000196 N. Maximum free-node force was 0.07156 N forward and 0.01121 N reversed. The latter still exceeds the 0.01 N criterion, and neither case validates coupled force transfer.

This is evidence of a severe ordering or order-induced parameterization sensitivity in the research path, consistent with but not proving that the ordered Gauss-Seidel sweep is the sole cause. A symmetric, converged formulation and a physical residual are required; selecting the better order would hide the defect. The straight rod uses identical symmetric masses and stiffnesses, but reversing its ordering also changes its material-frame parameterization, which this experiment cannot independently separate. Source, commands, binary/shader hashes, states and forces are retained under `evidence/crow-feather-rachis-20261116/node-order/`. The safety gate was bypassed solely for this rejected prototype and restored afterward.
